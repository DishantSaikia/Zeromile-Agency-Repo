# Trustworthy Design: 4 Credibility Factors

Source: Nielsen Norman Group, Aurora Harley — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/
Published: 2016-05-08
Retrieved: 2026-07-17

## The four factors

1. **Design quality.** Professional appearance via organized structure,
   appropriate color, and high-quality imagery. "Typos, broken links, and
   other mistakes quickly degrade credibility" — especially damaging on
   transaction-focused sites where users are evaluating reliability before
   sharing contact/payment information.

2. **Upfront disclosure.** Transparent, visible: contact information,
   pricing (including fees), delivery/availability windows, return
   policy/guarantees. A cited participant on a service provider that hid
   rates: "I would definitely not use them because they don't state the
   rate here, they want us to actually write to them. So I feel they are
   not open enough." The article separately warns that login/registration
   walls placed before any value is shown damage trust.

3. **Comprehensive, correct, current content.** Detailed accurate
   information demonstrating real expertise — for service-booking sites,
   showing the actual range of services and real process photos (not just
   idealized end results) so users can assess who they're dealing with.

4. **Connection to the rest of the web.** External validation: social
   presence, independent reviews, third-party mentions. Participants
   "trust these external sources more than company-sponsored content."

## Relevance to this project (Zeromile Agency)

- Upfront disclosure: the site already shows per-vehicle/stay pricing
  without a login wall and never gates content behind signup — matches
  the rule. Good.
- Design quality / current content: vehicle and stay photos are currently
  placehold.co placeholders, not real photos. Per factor 3, real process
  photos materially affect trust for a service where strangers hand over
  car keys/house keys — this is a live gap, not just cosmetic, and should
  be flagged to the business owner as a priority once photos exist (already
  noted as a to-do in the codebase's image data layer).
- Connection to the rest of the web / external validation: the
  testimonials on the homepage are placeholder/fictional copy. Per factor
  4, users trust independent validation *more* than company-sponsored
  content — meaning placeholder testimonials, once real customer
  quotes/reviews exist, are a stronger trust lever than almost any visual
  polish. Flagged as a content gap, not a design gap.
